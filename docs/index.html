VetServe

Straight answers about benefits, and neighbors who’ll help with the rest.

Built by Sharvin Goyal and Tharun Parasaram — a web app that answers veterans’ benefits questions using only official government documents, with every sentence cited, and connects veterans with verified student volunteers for everyday help.

Two halves, one community

Veterans face a maze of federal and state benefits — and the everyday tasks that pile up around them. Students want to serve, but service hours often turn into busywork. VetServe connects the two.

VetGuide

Ask about eligibility, disability claims, health care, or education benefits in plain language. Answers are built only from official documents — VA handbooks, benefits pages, real office directories — and every sentence carries an inline citation that expands to the exact source passage.

The task board

Veterans post small tasks — a ride to the VA clinic, help with a phone, yard work. School-verified student volunteers claim them, the veteran chooses whom to accept, and confirmed help becomes service hours a school can verify with one code.

Built to not make things up

A wrong answer about benefits isn’t a typo — it’s someone’s health care. So trust is engineered in, not promised:

  • Citation validator. After every answer, a validator deletes any sentence it cannot trace to a retrieved official passage — and logs what it removed.
  • Honest refusals. If the documents don’t cover a question, VetServe says so and shows the phone number of a real office instead of guessing.
  • Crisis handling. Language that sounds like a crisis never reaches the AI; the screen immediately becomes the Veterans Crisis Line (988, press 1).
  • Privacy in the database. A veteran’s address is visible to exactly one accepted volunteer — enforced by row-level security, not interface rules.
  • Verifiable hours. Service-hour PDFs carry a code and QR that resolve against the database, so the paper can’t be forged.

How it’s built

Free and open source end to end — no paid services between a veteran and their answer. The whole stack runs on one laptop.
